Language and literacy

Language and literacy is about how children communicate and make meaning. It includes spoken language, sign, Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC) and written language. Strong language skills help children express their thoughts, ideas and feelings. They also lay the foundation for learning to read and write. Language and literacy skills develop over time and are shaped by who children are, their identity, culture, the language they speak at home, and their daily experiences.

Families are children’s first teachers. They shape language from birth and help children build knowledge, identity and connection.  

Overview

In the early years, children begin to explore sounds, words and print. Learning experiences like rhyming, storytelling, books and recognising their name in print help to build phonological awareness (sounds in words) and phonics skills (the sounds associated with letters in print). These early experiences support literacy development and help children become confident communicators.

Language and literacy learning should be culturally responsive, inclusive and suited to each child’s age and stage. Everyday experiences with language and literacy build strong communication skills and support overall development. They also spark joy, curiosity and a love of reading. These experiences help children feel confident, capable and proud.

First languages shape how children see and describe the world. They connect children to family, identity and culture. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children may be developing skills across one or more languages when they begin early learning. A child’s home language should be recognised, respected and supported.

Teachers and educators can build language and literacy by collaborating with families, Elders and community members. Story, song, art and dance support meaningful language learning. Strategies include sharing Dreaming stories, using first language words, scribing children’s ideas, offering visual supports and encouraging talk through play.

Queensland is home to many distinct Aboriginal languages and Torres Strait Islander languages. Each language belongs to a group of people who are its custodians. When we respect and include these languages in early learning, we help keep them strong and strengthen identity, culture and connection for all children. We also create respectful, inclusive environments where all children feel seen, heard and valued.
