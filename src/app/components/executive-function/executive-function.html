Executive function

Executive function is a set of skills that support children to manage impulses, focus attention, adapt to change and work towards goals. These skills help children to remember instructions, switch between tasks and keep trying when things get tricky. Executive functions skills support learning and development across all areas and are shaped by each child’s cultural, social and emotional experiences.

Families are children’s first teachers, so executive function skills are influenced by family routines, relationships and community values. Recognising the whole child means seeing these relationships as central to their learning, wellbeing and identity. For Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children, awareness of their kinship networks and connections to Country are important to developing identity, which supports growth in executive function skills.

Overview

In the early years, children begin building the foundations for executive function. These skills support learning, relationships and wellbeing. They help children manage everyday challenges and grow as confident, capable learners.  They include working memory, inhibitory control and cognitive flexibility. While we can’t see these brain processes directly, we can notice them through behaviours like persistence, problem solving and adaptability.  

Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander ways of learning offer inclusive and holistic approaches to developing executive function. Learning through story, song, art, dance and connection to Country encourages children to plan, reflect and adapt.  Strategies may include traditional storytelling and songs that build planning, organisation and decision-making; traditional art that nurtures attention to detail; and traditional games that strengthen flexible thinking and teamwork. When we invite Elders and community members to share cultural knowledge, stories and experiences, we create rich, respectful learning environments that strengthen executive function and celebrate diverse ways of knowing, being and belonging.
