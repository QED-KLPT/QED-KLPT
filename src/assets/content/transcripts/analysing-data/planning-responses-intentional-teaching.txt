- Language and literacy focuses on how children use words through speech, sign, or augmentative and alternative
communication, and literacy for expressive and
receptive communication. In order to develop language and literacy skills,
children need to develop their proficiency in using
and understanding words. Language and literacy in
early childhood involves foundational skills, such as
recognising letters and sounds, and understanding the connection between spoken, sign,
and written language. It encompasses comprehending stories and understanding different
narrative structures. Language and literacy can be categorised into two key elements: sounds and speech, which incorporates vocabulary,
sentence structure, and coherence and comprehension
and narrative awareness. Let's look at how children
develop their speech and sounds. When children use words to communicate, they need to understand
and use vocabulary, sentence structure, and grammar. They also need to create
clear and coherent sentences and texts. Children need awareness of
the sounds in spoken words to pronounce them accurately and understand written words
for reading and writing. Teachers and educators
can support children to develop sounds and speech by explaining and giving clear examples to help children connect sounds in words, and encourage active listening.
Active listening helps children develop auditory discrimination skills. It provides them with an opportunity to identify and differentiate variances in speech.
Now, let's dive deeper into comprehension and narrative awareness. Language is fundamentally
about conveying meaning, and comprehension is key
to understanding language. It is important for children
to be able to comprehend and make sense of
information they receive, and in turn, convey meaning
to express themselves. As children grow, they
learn to use language in everyday conversations, as well as more advanced
skills to understand and discuss a range of texts read to them. Children can show their
understanding of writing by making marks. We gather evidence of their understanding that these marks hold
meaning when they talk about what their writing says. This shows development in their language and understanding of narrative structure.
So, how can teachers and educators support children's learning and development in this area? Open-ended questions
can encourage children to think about the sounds they hear and the words they are learning. This supports experimentation
with sounds and speech, and promotes critical thinking. We can support children's comprehension by using intentional teaching strategies. These strategies highlight
new ideas and topics, leading to improved
comprehension of the content, and context and connection
to experiences and ideas.
