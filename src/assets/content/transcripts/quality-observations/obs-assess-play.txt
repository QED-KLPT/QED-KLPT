The Early Years Learning Framework Version 2 describes assessment as an ongoing cycle that includes observing, analysing, planning, and critical reflection.
Assessment for children's learning, also known as formative assessment, provides the foundation for informing planning and pedagogy.
Gathering evidence of what children know, can do and understand enables teachers and educators to intentionally consider ways they can build on children's existing knowledge and skills and progress towards learning outcomes.
Through quality observations, teachers and educators build a picture of the unique strengths, learning dispositions, skills, and understandings of each child.
The Queensland Kindergarten Learning Progressions are an evidence-based instrument that connects to the Queensland Kindergarten Learning Guideline and the Early Years Learning Framework Version Two.
It provides a framework for strengthening the practice of making observations and supports intentional teaching strategies to develop children's learning.
As part of the assessment and planning cycle, the Learning Progressions enable teachers and educators to plan, act, and critically reflect on the evidence of children's learning collected through quality observations in everyday contexts.
Teachers and educators can observe children's early learning and development as progressions within 5 core skills.
These include language and literacy, executive function, social and emotional learning, physicality, and mathematics and numeracy.
The language and literacy progression focuses on how children use words through speech, sign, or Augmentative and Alternative Communication, AAC, in order to share their thoughts and understand what others say, sign, or write.
Executive function is a set of skills that enable children to control impulses, sustain focus, and achieve goals.
These skills are important for learning. They develop during early childhood and include working memory, inhibitory control, and cognitive flexibility.
Social learning involves children learning in pairs, small groups, or as a whole group.
Collaborative learning opportunities in early childhood help the development of children's communication skills, social skills, social regulation skills, and awareness of others.
Emotional learning begins in infancy and continues into adolescence.
Children learn to identify and regulate their emotions and understand the emotions of others.
Physical learning is the skills and abilities that support children to learn to move with stability, control, and awareness of their environment, and to manage the control of objects.
The physicality core skill addresses gross motor development, fine motor development, and integrates consideration of sensory learning.
The mathematics and numeracy progressions relate to children's ability to understand, describe, discuss, and think about their environment.
This progression describes the development of numbers for counting and understanding quantity.
It supports the use of rich language to talk and think about concepts of time, length, distance, capacity, and area in everyday activities.
