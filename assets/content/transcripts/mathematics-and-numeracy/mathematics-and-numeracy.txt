Mathematics and numeracy relates to how children talk about and understand their environment.
This progression describes important numeracy skills, like counting and understanding quantity.
It supports children to use rich language to talk about time, length, distance, capacity, and area in everyday life.
When children are engaged in meaningful mathematics and numeracy experiences, they're developing their thinking skills by exploring their environment, asking questions, posing problems, predicting, testing, and reflecting.
This supports and develops skills in early science, technology and mathematics.
Mathematics and numeracy can be categorised in the following ways: counting, numbering, and quantifying; sorting, patterning; using spatial and measurement language.
Counting, numbering and quantifying are foundational skills that empower children to understand and engage with the world around them, learning and being curious throughout everyday life.
They are essential, fundamental building blocks for recognising and understanding mathematical concepts.
These skills enhance cognitive development and language development.
Children represent their understanding of counting and quantity through actions, drawings and creations.
Using games, puzzles, and connecting counting to real-world situations like shopping or reading engages children through relevance and aligns with their interests, encouraging them to practice these skills in real life.
Sorting, recognising patterns and using spatial and measurement language are integral in early childhood development, contributing to academic and everyday life success.
They help develop cognitive skills, such as categorisation, classification, and problem solving.
